# Four-coin breakout pilot

This exploratory replay checks the direction of the proposed four-READY filter using public Delta India testnet five-minute trade and mark candles for 21 October to 3 November 2026 UTC. It is a development sample. Use a later untouched period for a promotion decision.

Each five-minute decision reconstructs the closed-bar breakout from the preceding 20 highs and positive mean historical volume. Turnover ranking uses the previous 24 hours of traded candle volume and prices as a proxy. Both rules use a four-coin batch, three contracts per coin, the same ten-minute maximum hold, mark-price stop and target, a $10 planned position loss cap, $30 daily loss allowance and an 80% margin budget. The planner uses margin at the 5× ceiling for feasibility. Each synthetic UTC day starts with $160.7083. Net P&L updates that day's cash, and the next batch in each arm waits ten minutes even if a bracket exits sooner. No model trade crosses a UTC-hour boundary.

Entry and timed exit proxies use the first traded price in the corresponding five-minute candle. A bracket uses mark-price highs and lows. When both thresholds appear in one candle, the stop takes precedence, and an adverse open can worsen the stop. Fees are 5.9 basis points each way. Execution adds one contemporary observed half-spread estimate by symbol plus an assumed 1 basis point of extra cost per side. The stress case uses 5 basis points of extra cost. Actual historic spreads, depth and fills are unavailable. Day limits here use UTC and exclude funding by omitting hour-crossing windows.

Only 860 of 3,360 potentially eligible decision windows had complete five-coin data and positive traded volume in every coin's entry and deadline proxy candle. Twenty-five retained windows contained at least four READY coins before portfolio and overlap checks. The complete-data filter is a coverage limitation, including its use of the deadline bar's activity to find a usable price. Do not treat the replay cadence or results as a forecast.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| WAIT allowed, base costs | 414 | 1,656 | 17.69% | −0.1583 |
| Four READY, base costs | 19 | 76 | 36.84% | −0.1424 |

| Four READY, stressed costs | 19 | 76 | 22.37% | −0.2567 |

Four READY increased the descriptive win rate in this replay. Both rules lost money after modeled costs, and the READY arm's small base-cost advantage in average loss reversed under stressed costs. Scarce complete windows, shared market exposure across four positions and different activity rates prevent a causal or independent profitability conclusion. Treat the filter as a candidate for prospective validation, with quote-level execution and after-cost comparison.

## Prospective data capture

The first reviewed group registered `delta-demo-trade1-four-ready-v1`. Its frozen 84-day window runs from 20 November 2026 at 00:00 IST through 12 February 2027 at 00:00 IST, exclusive. The read-only `crypto_skills.delta_candidate_capture` process uses public India testnet market endpoints. Each five-minute record contains the closed-candle evidence, ticker and five levels of order-book depth for the same five symbols. Each record is keyed by the decision boundary in `four-ready-quotes.sqlite3`. Capture lag, stale observations and missing slots must remain visible in the coverage report. The test database contains zero qualifying records before the start.

The capture provides observed prices and available depth at a point in time. Replay still needs explicit protective-trigger, exit-price, funding and no-fill handling and a matched evaluation against the frozen baseline. The process has no exchange order capability.

The entry replay uses the existing Trade 1 batch planner and the pre-window testnet contract specifications in `candidate-input-products.json`. Both arms apply the same cash, day-loss and order limits to the same timestamp. The four-READY arm only considers aligned, confirmed breakouts. The model walks the recorded offer levels up to each IOC limit and identifies full, partial, zero or unresolved displayed-depth results. A fill projected from a book snapshot can differ from an exchange fill if the book changes between observation and submission.

The timed-exit replay requires a captured bid within 15 seconds after the exact ten-minute deadline, full visible close quantity and continuous one-minute mark candles. It calculates fees on both fills and requires a documented funding cashflow, including documented zero where applicable. Possible stop or target touches remain unresolved until separate protective-trigger and executable-price evidence establishes their outcomes. Missing close evidence cannot be counted as zero P&L or a completed modeled trade.

The frame assembler joins each SQLite decision snapshot with the deadline snapshot, exact per-coin book observation time, reference mark minutes and signed funding per contract. Missing funding stays unknown. The portfolio replay processes those frames in five-minute order and withholds new openings until the modeled account is flat. It carries forward cash and charges realized losses to the IST settlement date. Settled partial fills affect cash and risk capacity, and the full three-contract performance sample is counted separately. An unresolved exposure leaves subsequent portfolio results incomplete. The final ten-minute interval is reserved for settlements. Recorded mark history, funding attribution and evidence for protective fills are still needed to settle the prospective replay.

From the project root, `python -m crypto_skills.delta_candidate_replay` checks the frozen identity and tape coverage read-only. With a settlement-evidence JSON argument it replays both baseline and four-READY through a completed IST date on the same frames, starting balance and fee assumption. The evidence object includes `version`, `evidence_sha256`, `completed_through_ist`, `cash`, `fee_rate`, one-minute `marks` keyed by symbol, and `funding_per_contract` keyed by entry slot then symbol. Each arm runs at base and stressed execution costs. Stress debits the observed adverse midpoint-to-fill cost once more on each side, applying the extra debit to subsequent cash and IST daily risk. Fees and recorded book fills are held fixed for each tested opportunity. Independent protective and funding evidence is still required.

Once the two shadow portfolios have settled results, run `python -m crypto_skills.delta_candidate_score PAIRED_OUTCOMES.json` from the project root. The replay output has the frozen identity and one row per date. The portfolio keys `baseline`, `candidate`, `stressed_baseline` and `stressed_candidate` list the full-size trade results. Their `_settled_net_usd` fields include resolved partials for account P&L and drawdown. Include every date, including idle days. Keep quote gaps and unsettled or ambiguous trades in the upstream evidence so incomplete days cannot qualify.

The scorer calculates both primary outcome differences using the same resampled seven-day blocks in both arms, measures drawdown and checks the higher-cost comparison. A passing score still requires review of source coverage and simulation assumptions before any rule can be deployed.

## Public exit data checks

On 19 November 2026 around 02:56 IST, India testnet REST returned all 14 requested consecutive, closed `MARK:` one-minute bars for each of BTCUSD, SOLUSD, ETHUSD, DOGEUSD and XRPUSD. The same recent minute range returned no `FUNDING:` rows, while longer ranges returned historical rate observations. The REST tickers provided a rate for all five and no next-realization timestamp in this check. Historical rate observations alone cannot establish a funding payment or zero funding for a modeled position. [Delta API documentation](https://docs.delta.exchange/)

A 50-second pre-window probe of the India testnet public WebSocket received 98 `ob_l2` snapshots and 25–26 `candlestick_1m` mark updates per symbol. The largest observed gaps were 501 ms for books and 2.033 seconds for mark updates. `funding_rate` messages carried the next realization and an interval of one hour for DOGEUSD and eight hours for BTCUSD, ETHUSD and SOLUSD. XRPUSD sent no funding message in this probe. Funding coverage needs to be tracked per symbol and across reconnects. [Delta public WebSocket](https://docs.delta.exchange/#websocket-feed) · [Delta funding schedule](https://www.delta.exchange/support/solutions?articleId=80001183756&categoryId=80000464977&folderId=80000726949)

These observations are source checks from before the frozen window. A five-minute decision snapshot can arrive earlier than the previous snapshot's capture offset plus ten minutes, leaving no eligible close book. A continuous book stream can resolve that timing gap and supply books around protective triggers. It still requires checks for stale data, trigger ordering, sufficient depth and funding at the modeled exposure boundaries. Leave missing settlements unresolved and exclude these probes from the candidate score.

## Funding schedule recorder

Run `python -m crypto_skills.delta_candidate_capture --funding-node <node-executable>` to collect public testnet funding updates for the five campaign symbols. The recorder uses the registered candidate's exact validation window and appends one JSON line per accepted observation to `four-ready-funding-YYYY-MM-DD.jsonl` in this directory. The date in each file name is UTC. Each row carries its registration digest, exchange time, local receipt time, next funding realization, interval, observed rate and stream ID. A stream interruption or a missing symbol remains visible as a gap.

Add `--smoke` for a short read-only source check that reports observed updates without writing validation rows. `STOP_CANDIDATE_FUNDING` stops the recorder. [Delta public funding stream](https://docs.delta.exchange/#funding_rate)

The settlement replay reads the same registered funding rows up to its cutoff. It can model zero funding when qualifying observations bracket both exact holding times, come from one stream, and agree on the same next funding timestamp and interval. Observed and receipt timestamps must be monotonic with no gap larger than 120 seconds. The whole position must fit strictly between the two schedule boundaries. Gaps, reconnects and holds that reach a funding event leave the amount unresolved unless an independently supplied signed cashflow covers that position. Actual exchange charges are reconciled from fills and wallet postings.

## Deadline order-book recorder

Run `python -m crypto_skills.delta_candidate_capture --exit-books` alongside the decision recorder. It reads the frozen entry's capture time, adds the 600-second hold and requests a five-level public testnet book for each campaign coin two seconds after that deadline. It stores one record per entry slot in the `deadline_books` table of `four-ready-quotes.sqlite3`. The replay uses the earliest eligible book delivered within 15 seconds after the deadline and requires the exchange snapshot timestamp to be at or after the deadline. Missing or stale books leave the timer exit unresolved unless another captured book meets the same checks.

`--exit-books --smoke` checks the public source without writing research observations. Use `STOP_CANDIDATE_EXIT_BOOKS` to stop only this recorder. Simulated closes still depend on valid displayed depth, mark-path coverage and funding. A possible earlier protective trigger needs its own execution-price evidence.

## Closed mark-price history

Use `python -m crypto_skills.delta_candidate_replay SETTLEMENT_EVIDENCE.json --fetch-marks` to fill the mark input from the public testnet `MARK:` history endpoint for the requested completed IST dates. The replay reports expected, observed and missing minutes for each symbol. Out-of-range, duplicate and invalid bars leave their page unresolved. A missing minute remains absent and can block a modeled exit. The explicit `marks` input remains available for an audit replay with preserved data.

A pre-window November 19 check returned 1,440 distinct closed mark bars per coin across all five coins for one completed IST day. The public endpoint also supplied a full 1,440-minute UTC day per coin approximately 84 days earlier. Those source checks are outside the frozen performance sample. ## Protective-exit source checks

A 42-second public testnet probe on November 19 paired 101 of 103 sampled mark updates with a same-symbol book carrying a later or equal exchange timestamp. The largest observed pairing delay was 501 ms. Mark samples were about two seconds apart and books about half a second apart. ETHUSD and XRPUSD each had one unmatched sample. The feed can preserve an observed trigger interval and nearby displayed quotes. A possible exchange trigger between samples still needs ordered evidence and a modeled price range. [Delta public mark-price stream](https://docs.delta.exchange/#mark_price) · [Delta public order book](https://docs.delta.exchange/#ob_l2)

A separate 45-second sample retained five bid levels, the best offer, mark updates and partial mark candles in a compact format. Its 670 rows took 96,603 bytes before gzip and 9,265 bytes after. Extrapolating this sample gives roughly 1.5 GB compressed for 84 days. The observed rate is a sizing estimate. A recorder should use bounded compressed chunks, retain exchange and receipt timestamps, and expose feed gaps and available disk capacity before treating an interval as covered.

Run `python -m crypto_skills.delta_candidate_capture --protection-node <node-executable>` to collect that public evidence. The recorder writes atomic gzip chunks in `runs/trade1/protective-quotes/YYYY-MM-DD` by UTC date. Each chunk holds at most five minutes from one connection and includes the frozen registration and symbol order. Row types are `mark`, `book` and `candle`. Each carries a symbol index, exchange timestamp in microseconds and local receipt timestamp in milliseconds. Books carry their last update timestamp, five bids and the best offer. Partial candles carry the exchange candle start in microseconds and OHLC values.

The recorder stops writing if local free space falls below 4 GiB. It begins observations at the frozen start and stops at the registered end or `STOP_CANDIDATE_PROTECTION`. `--smoke` checks the three live channels on all five symbols without saving observations. A separate isolated live record test read back 123 observations from one valid compressed chunk across all 15 symbol and channel combinations.

Candidate replay uses the archive when a full reference minute flags one possible protective trigger. Two consecutive same-stream partial candles must bracket that first crossing after entry, at most three seconds apart. The book series must extend from just before the possible trigger through at least half a second after the crossing update, with gaps no greater than one second and full displayed quantity at each sampled book. The model uses the lowest displayed sell proceeds in that range and releases cash at the last sampled exchange-book timestamp. Consistent public funding observations must establish zero funding through that modeled close. Ambiguous triggers, incomplete coverage and insufficient depth remain unresolved. The result is a modeled exit under sampled liquidity. Actual protective fills require exchange order and wallet evidence.

## Replay memory and input consistency

All four arms read one temporary SQLite backup of the quote tape. The replay keeps one IST day's decision frames in memory and can read the next ten minutes of snapshots for exits across midnight. Portfolio cash, position timing and daily risk accounting carry forward through the whole replay. Mark history and funding schedules retain their indexes for the run.

In an isolated seven-day benchmark with 2,016 synthetic decision slots, full-frame and day-by-day output had identical SHA-256 digests. Peak traced Python allocations dropped from 135.5 MiB to 19.5 MiB. The benchmark took 14.96 seconds with full loading and 17.36 seconds with day-by-day loading. It excluded API download time, protective archive reads and a full 84-day portfolio run.

Daily replay output includes execution counts for each arm. Scheduled slots are divided into slots occupied by an earlier modeled position, observed frames, the first missing frame when applicable, and slots left unreplayed after an unresolved decision or settlement. An observed frame can still be incomplete. Order counts record planned lots, modeled IOC attempts, unsent lots and each IOC outcome, including zero fill and unresolved depth. These diagnostics preserve the difference between an idle decision and missing evidence without changing the promotion criteria.
